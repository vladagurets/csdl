# Methodological analysis: El Lissitzky, *About Two Squares* / *Two Squares*

The source PDF was provided privately for analysis and is intentionally not committed to this repository. CSDL adopts compositional methods, not historical or political styling.

## Page-sequence observations

- **Pages 1–2:** high-contrast framing and typography establish a rule system before the narrative begins.
- **Pages 3–5:** a very small number of elements gain exceptional weight through active negative space, off-center placement, and large scale differences.
- **Pages 4–9:** geometric forms behave as actors and forces. They enter, collide, fragment, reorganize, and resolve; geometry carries narrative rather than decorating text.
- **Pages 6–8:** the most explosive spreads are effective because they follow quieter pages. Repeating this density on every page would remove the climax.
- **Page 10:** resolution compresses the story into a very small central sign surrounded by silence.
- **Page 13:** a restrained final mark creates closure with minimal means.

## Principles adopted by CSDL

1. Space is active content.
2. Geometry can function as noun, verb, and relation.
3. Asymmetry remains controlled by a clear hierarchy.
4. Color carries semantic responsibility.
5. A sequence needs quiet and loud states.
6. Typography may become form only when readability survives.
7. Scale contrast creates memory more reliably than decorative variety.

## Explicitly rejected

- Soviet and revolutionary symbols;
- propaganda language;
- imitation of 1920s lettering;
- routine red/black wedges as a decorative signature;
- distressed historical print texture;
- political associations;
- artfulness that obscures the information.

## Translation into Quiet Modular

CSDL emphasizes the quieter logic of the book—especially the sparse compositions and final resolution—more than its most chaotic spreads. A typical card therefore contains one idea, one visual mechanism, one signal color, and 50–75% negative space. Strong collision or poster-scale planes are reserved for rare Level C moments.
